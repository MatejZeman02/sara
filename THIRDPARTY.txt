Libraries compiled into the native modules
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two modules are native libraries built beside the binary, the JPEG XL codec
(sara-jxl, libsara-jxl.*.so and .dll) and the TIFF reader's LZW unpacker
(libsara-tiff-lzw.*.so and .dll). Both are built from the modules'
CMakeLists.txt and link everything statically, so the only libraries they
load are the system's own: libc, libm and the loader on Linux, KERNEL32 and
the Universal C Runtime's api-ms-win-crt parts on Windows. What is compiled
into them is:

Krita 5.2.13
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Copyright (c) 1999-2022 the Krita contributors, named in the copyright lines
the five Sara files below carry verbatim, listed one page below.
License: GNU General Public License v2.0 or later, except
         libs/pigment/compositeops/KoCompositeOpAlphaDarken.h and
         plugins/paintops/libpaintop/kis_texture_option.cpp, which are
         GNU Lesser General Public License v2.0 or later. Both permit
         relicensing upwards, and Sara's own licence is GPLv3-or-later.
Source: https://invent.kde.org/graphics/krita

Sara's stamp brush engine and its dynamics were written against Krita 5.2.13.
Almost all of it is written from what the mechanisms do rather than from Krita's
code. Five ports remain, and each carries the original SPDX-FileCopyrightText
lines verbatim, unmodified, beside Sara's own SPDX-License-Identifier.

They are listed, with their lines and the mechanism each is for, in four
files, the stamp brush's for the one it still holds, the door's two for the
three that moved into it, the sensor and the stroke state with the brush's
dynamics and the tip image with the tips, and the widget
toolkit's for the one that moved with the curve option control:

  modules/sara-base-modules/stamp-brush/PROVENANCE.md
  core/api/dynamics/PROVENANCE.md
  core/api/tip/PROVENANCE.md
  ui/widgets/curve_option/PROVENANCE.md

The five Sara files they name are:

  core/api/tip/sa_tip_image.gd
  core/api/dynamics/sa_sensor.gd
  core/api/dynamics/speed/sa_speed_smoother.gd
  ui/widgets/curve_option/sa_curve_option_control.gd
  modules/sara-base-modules/stamp-brush/shaders/pattern_modes.gmacs

All five carry a pointer to the page beside them rather than the lines
themselves, which is a rule of its own: a licence obligation spread over eight
headers in two directories is one refactor from losing a line and nobody
noticing, and that was found when two files credited the wrong person
for the same mechanism. The fourth was the brush editor's and kept its lines in
its own header until it moved into the module and then on into the widget
toolkit.

Two suites read those pages on every run of the test suite and fail when a file
carries a line its page does not grant it, or when a page grants a line to a
file that does not exist.
modules/sara-base-modules/stamp-brush/tests/test_provenance.gd sweeps the
module against its page and sara-godot/tests/test_dynamics_provenance.gd sweeps
the skeleton's three directories, ui/brush_editor, ui/widgets and core, against
the door's two.

Everything else was read against Krita's behaviour rather than taken. The
project's two brush specifications, the stamp engine's and the dynamics', keep
a row per mechanism naming the Krita source it was read against and whether it
was ported or only looked at, and a public build does not carry them. A
mechanism that was only read owes nobody anything. The ports above are the
whole of what was copied.

Krita 5.2.13, the Smart Patch tool's numbers in the patch tool
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Copyright (c) 2017 Eugene Ingerman, as the file's own SPDX-FileCopyrightText says
License: GNU General Public License v2.0 or later, which permits relicensing
         upwards, and Sara's own licence is GPLv3-or-later.
Source: https://invent.kde.org/graphics/krita,
        plugins/tools/tool_smart_patch/kis_inpaint.cpp

The patch tool's Texture method was written for the GPU from PatchMatch
(Barnes, Shechtman, Finkelstein and Goldman, 2009) and the completion of
Wexler, Shechtman and Irani (2007), and copies no line of Krita's file. It
keeps Krita's tuned numbers: the similarity curve and its 0.999 and 0.10, the
distance's scale, the floor under a vote, the patch radius of four and the
rounds per level. The lines and what each number is are on

  modules/sara-base-modules/base/patch/PROVENANCE.md

and the two Sara files it names are

  modules/sara-base-modules/base/patch/patch_match.gmacs
  modules/sara-base-modules/base/patch/sa_patch_match.gd

Krita's file names Xavier Philippeau as the original author and David
Chatting's PatchMatch (https://github.com/davidchatting/PatchMatch) as the code
it adopted. Sara takes nothing from either beyond what Krita ships.

OpenDRT v1.1.0, the Tonemap filter's OpenDRT method
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Written by Jed Smith
License: GNU General Public License v3.0
Source: https://github.com/jedypod/open-display-transform,
        display-transforms/opendrt/OpenDRT.dctl, v1.1.0

The Tonemap filter offers two methods: Sara's own Paper, made for a painter's
canvas, and OpenDRT, a display transform made to form a picture out of
scene-linear camera data. OpenDRT is Jed Smith's, ported from the DCTL to a
Sara kernel, and the DCTL's own banner is carried verbatim in

  modules/sara-base-modules/base/shaders/opendrt.gmacs

The repository's parameters page is what the filter's rows, their ranges and
the Look and Tonescale presets were read from. The DCTL's header writes
"License: GPLv3" with no copyright line, which is read as GPL-3.0, and Sara's
own licence is GPLv3-or-later, so the port ships under it. The Sara files
that carry it are

  modules/sara-base-modules/base/shaders/opendrt.gmacs
  modules/sara-base-modules/base/usual/opendrt_parameters.gd
  modules/sara-base-modules/base/usual/tonemap_filter.gd

Pictures, tips, icons and profiles made for Sara
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Sara project made the following itself, and they are under the project's
licence, the GNU General Public License v3.0 or later, except where a line
below says otherwise. None of them is taken from another project.

Interface icons (sara-godot/assets/icons/), drawn for Sara on a 16 by 16 grid.
Some names follow the names of Godot's editor icons because the interface once
fell back to the editor's theme, and the shapes are Sara's own.

The application icon (sara-godot/ui/icon.png, icon-bw.png and icon.ico), a 3D
render of Sara's mark made for the project, with its black and white cut.

The colour profiles (sara-godot/assets/icc/), written by

the notice "No copyright, use freely".

The welcome pictures (sara-godot/ui/welcome/pictures/), painted in Sara, by
hand and by coding agents driving it through its door, and exported by Sara
with its own sRGB profile.

The manual's pictures (sara-godot/manual/pictures/) and the boot cover
(sara-godot/assets/boot_cover_stock.webp), shots of Sara. Two of the manual's
pictures show Krita's CC0 preset bundle, named above.

The brush tips (sara-godot/tips/). Twenty-three are drawings made for Sara,
and each PNG carries its own notice in its text chunks, the Creative Commons
Attribution 4.0 International licence (CC BY 4.0) with the attribution to
travel with the file. The other tips are shapes made for the test suites and
are under the project's licence.

The test pictures under the tests folders and sara-godot/scenarios/fixtures
are written by the suites' scripts or exported by Sara, and are under the
project's licence too.
